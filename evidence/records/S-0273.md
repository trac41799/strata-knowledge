Large-N scaling study: trains 400+ transformer language models across parameter
counts, dataset sizes and compute budgets to derive compute-optimal scaling
laws. Finds model size and training tokens should scale at equal rates (~20
tokens per parameter at the compute-optimal point), correcting Kaplan et al.
(2020), which held that parameter count should grow faster than data. Validates
the recipe by training Chinchilla (70B parameters, 1.4T tokens, same compute
budget as Gopher): it outperforms Gopher (280B), GPT-3 (175B), Jurassic-1
(178B) and Megatron-Turing NLG (530B) on a large range of downstream
evaluations. Chinchilla is an autoregressive transformer (Gopher architecture).
Verified via arXiv abstract page and secondary analyses (Epoch AI replication
note; Wikipedia).
