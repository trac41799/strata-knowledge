Industrial case study: since 2011, engineers at Amazon Web Services (AWS)
have used TLA+ (formal specification) with the TLC model checker (and
PlusCal) to solve difficult design problems in critical distributed systems,
beginning with DynamoDB and S3. The practice is design-level: engineers write
models of system designs — "exhaustively testable pseudo-code" — and model
check them, finding serious, subtle bugs before production that were not
found by any other technique, and enabling aggressive optimization of complex
algorithms without sacrificing quality. Teams were able to learn TLA+ from
scratch and adopt it in normal engineering practice; the paper reports that
teams which used it found high value and that adoption spread. Caveats stated
in the paper: formal methods deal with models of systems, not the systems
themselves — "all models are wrong, some are useful" — so the specification
must capture the significant aspects of the real system. Verified against the
ACM DOI record (10.1145/2699417, CACM 58(4):66-73) and the full paper text
(companion technical report, September 2014).
