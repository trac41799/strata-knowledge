The final NIST standard specifying ML-KEM, a key-encapsulation mechanism
(KEM) derived from the CRYSTALS-Kyber submission, for establishing shared
secret keys over public channels. The security of ML-KEM is related to the
computational difficulty of the Module Learning with Errors (MLWE) problem,
and NIST states it is believed secure even against adversaries who possess a
quantum computer. The standard specifies three parameter sets: ML-KEM-512,
ML-KEM-768, and ML-KEM-1024, in increasing order of security strength and
decreasing performance. Published August 13, 2024, together with FIPS 204
(ML-DSA, derived from CRYSTALS-Dilithium) and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA, derived from
SPHINCS+) — the first finalized NIST post-quantum standards, concluding the
standardization process begun with NIST's 2016 call for candidates (announced
in the Federal Register, 2024-17956). Verified against the NIST CSRC
publication page (final, 08/13/2024) and the Federal Register notice.
