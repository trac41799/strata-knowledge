Industrial-scale study of contemporary peer review: Google projects
(Android, Chrome), Microsoft projects (Bing, Office, MS SQL Server), and
AMD, contrasted with traditional formal inspection at Lucent and
open-source peer review (Apache, Linux, KDE, FreeBSD, Gnome, Subversion).
Finding: review parameters converge across radically different settings
into a lightweight, tool-mediated practice — reviews start before the
change is committed (pre-commit), are picked up within hours, and
complete with median intervals on the order of hours to a day (AMD 17.5h;
Bing 14.7h; Office 18.9h; SQL 19.8h), versus ~10-day median intervals for
Lucent formal inspections. The rigidity of formal inspection limited its
adoption and efficiency. Introduces a knowledge-sharing measure: peer
review increased the number of distinct files a developer knows by
66-150% depending on the project. Web-verified via paper PDF and
Microsoft Research publication page (accessed 2026-08-19).
