Widely cited survey of the formal methods field: formal methods use
mathematical models for analysis and verification at any part of the program
life-cycle, covering the main technical families (model checking, theorem
proving, refinement-based development), industrial case studies and take-up
across safety-critical sectors, and the obstacles to wider adoption. Key
documented findings: the state-explosion problem — exponential growth of the
state space with system size — is the central scalability barrier for
model-checking-based verification; formal methods are typically applied to
critical components rather than whole systems; and cost, tooling maturity,
and the need for specialized expertise are the main obstacles reported by
industry. Verified against the ACM DOI record (10.1145/1592434.1592436,
ACM Computing Surveys 41(4)) and the full survey text.
