SEI report presenting the Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method (ATAM): a
stakeholder-driven technique that reveals how well an architecture satisfies
particular quality goals (e.g., performance, modifiability) and provides insight
into the tradeoffs involved. Outputs include sensitivity points (decisions where
a slight change makes a significant difference in a quality attribute), tradeoff
points (decisions affecting more than one quality attribute), and risks. The
method analyzes an architecture with respect to its quality attributes, not its
functional correctness. Web-verified: SEI library page (CMU/SEI-2000-TR-004,
August 2000); full-text PDF (TU Eindhoven mirror, wstomv.win.tue.nl); DTIC
technical note ADA388852 describing sensitivity/tradeoff definitions and the
non-correctness scope of ATAM.
