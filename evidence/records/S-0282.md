The seL4 microkernel (~8,700 lines of C, ~600 lines of assembly) was verified,
machine-checked in the Isabelle/HOL proof assistant, to be a functional
correct implementation of its abstract specification — the first such proof
for a complete, general-purpose operating-system kernel (original proof:
Klein et al., SOSP 2009, DOI 10.1145/1629575.1629596; research-highlights
overview: CACM 53(6), 2010). This 2014 survey article covers the full proof
stack: the functional correctness proof of the C implementation, a formally
verified IPC fastpath, a proof that the compiled binary correctly implements
the C semantics, a proof of correct access-control enforcement, a proof of
information-flow noninterference, and a sound worst-case execution time
analysis of the binary. The verification was made tractable by designing the
kernel for verification (small size, clean abstract specification). The
original proof assumed correctness of the compiler, assembly code, and
hardware; the binary-correctness proof closed the compiler gap. Verified
against the ACM DOI record and the seL4 publications list (sel4.systems,
trustworthy.systems).
