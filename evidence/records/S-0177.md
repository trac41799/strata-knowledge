Large-N observational study (peer-reviewed) of the long-term evolution of large
industrial software systems — most notably IBM's OS/360, using data first
acquired in a 1968 IBM study of the software process — that formulated the laws
of software evolution. Web-verified via Wikipedia ("Lehman's laws of software
evolution") and Lehman's own "Laws of Software Evolution Revisited" (1996):
Lehman and Belady formulated the first three laws in the mid-1970s; the 1980
Proceedings of the IEEE paper set down five laws; the list later grew to eight
(adding continuing growth, declining quality, and feedback-system laws by the
1990s). The 1980 paper also classifies programs into S-programs (spec-driven,
fully verifiable), P-programs (problem-driven), and E-programs (embedded in the
real world, which must evolve as the world changes) and argues the evolution
laws apply to E-type programs. Basis for the Lehman's-laws and S/P/E-taxonomy
claims of `operations/software-maintenance`.
