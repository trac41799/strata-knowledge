Foundational decomposition-criteria paper. Argues that the criteria used to
decompose a system into modules — not the module boundaries themselves —
determine changeability; proposes decomposing by "difficult design decisions"
and "design decisions which are likely to change" (information hiding).
Demonstrates with two decompositions of a KWIC index production system (by
processing steps vs. by hidden design decisions) and lists the benefits:
shortened development via parallel work, changeability, independent
compilation, and comprehensibility. Widely taught as the origin of
information hiding and of module-boundary design as a first-class activity.
