Internet Standard defining the web "origin": for hierarchical schemes the origin is
the (scheme, host, port) triple (Sec. 4), and two URIs are same-origin iff their
origins are identical (Sec. 5). The same-origin policy groups URIs into protection
domains: same-origin content interacts freely, while cross-origin interaction is
restricted — reading another origin's information is generally forbidden, sending
requests to it is permitted, and resources may opt into cross-origin reads (e.g.,
CORS, Sec. 3.4.2). Documents grant their origin's authority to the content they
render, so injecting untrusted content into a document "leaks" that authority — a
vulnerability named cross-site scripting (Sec. 3.3). The send-permitted /
read-forbidden asymmetry gives rise to cross-site request forgery (Sec. 3.4.3).
Cookies predate the origin model and use different units of isolation (Sec. 8.2).
