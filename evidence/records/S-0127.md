Formal foundation of event ordering in distributed systems. Defines the
happened-before partial order (program order within a process, send before
receive across processes, transitive closure) and proves that logical clocks
(monotonically increasing counters, incremented per event, carried in
messages, maxed on receive) satisfy the clock condition: a -> b implies
C(a) < C(b). Extends the partial order to an arbitrary total order by
breaking ties with process identifiers and demonstrates the replicated state
machine and distributed mutual exclusion as applications. Winner of the 2000
PODC Influential Paper Award (later the Dijkstra Prize) and ACM SIGOPS Hall
of Fame Award 2007. Basis for the T0 time-and-ordering claims of
`systems-software/distributed-systems-basics`.
