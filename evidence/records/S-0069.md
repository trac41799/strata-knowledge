Foundational position paper of the RISC movement: argues that the complex
instruction sets of the 1960s-70s (VAX, Intel 432) were motivated by code
density and immature compilers, and that simpler, load-store, register-based
instruction sets enable faster implementations, better pipelining, and more
efficient compiler use of registers. The thesis was contested at the time and
validated by the Berkeley RISC-I and Stanford MIPS projects.
