Widely adopted practitioner reference on distributed data systems. Chapter 3
(storage engines) covers how databases store data on disk: hash indexes and
log-structured (Bitcask-style) engines, SSTables and memtables, the B-tree vs
LSM-tree tradeoffs — write amplification, read amplification, compaction, and
Bloom filters for point lookups — the basis for the storage-structure claims of
`data/indexing-and-storage`. Chapter 5
(replication) covers single-leader replication, followers, failover, and
quorum-based read/write in leaderless replication. Chapter 8 ("The Trouble
with Distributed Systems") covers partial failure, the unreliable network
(packet loss, duplication, reordering, delay), timeout-based detection and
the crash-vs-slow indistinguishability, RPC and its leaky abstraction, and
unreliable clocks. Chapter 11 (stream processing) covers exactly-once
semantics and idempotence: exactly-once is never a transport property and is
achieved end-to-end via at-least-once delivery plus deduplication or
idempotent operations and atomic commit. Basis for the T3 failure-handling,
RPC, delivery-semantics, and replication-basics claims of
`systems-software/distributed-systems-basics`.
