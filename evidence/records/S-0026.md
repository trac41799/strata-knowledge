Empirical basis of the generational hypothesis: object-lifetime measurements
in Berkeley Smalltalk showed "young objects die young and old objects continue
to live". Replacing reference counting with two-generation scavenging cut the
time spent reclaiming storage 8-fold (13% → 1.5% of run time) and, after the
collector's intrinsic compaction removed an indirection table, the system ran
1.7x faster. Also introduces the remembered set for old-to-young references.
