Canonical survey defining an architectural style as a family of architectures:
a style prescribes a vocabulary of components and connectors (e.g., filters and
pipes), topological constraints (e.g., the graph must be acyclic), and semantic
constraints (e.g., filters cannot share state). Catalogues the pipes-and-filters,
data abstraction, implicit invocation (event-driven), layered, repository, and
distributed-processes (client-server, peer-to-peer) styles and argues that
selecting an appropriate architecture is crucial to a system's success — new
systems are built as variations on known families. Web-verified: full text PDF
at CMU (cs.cmu.edu, January 1994; also published as CMU/SEI-94-TR-21); UWaterloo
CS446 lecture slides reproduce the style catalogue and the implicit-invocation
(register handlers, announce events at runtime) description.
