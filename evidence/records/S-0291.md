NIST's transition plan for moving from quantum-vulnerable cryptographic
algorithms to post-quantum digital signature algorithms and key-establishment
schemes, identifying the vulnerable standards (RSA, ECC including P-256,
DSA/ECDSA) and the quantum-resistant targets (ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA). The
initial public draft proposes deprecating RSA-2048 and ECC P-256 by 2030
(no longer for new deployments) and disallowing quantum-vulnerable public-key
algorithms across NIST standards by 2035. The report explicitly treats the
harvest-now-decrypt-later (HNDL) scenario as an urgency driver for
confidentiality: adversaries can collect encrypted data today and decrypt it
once a cryptographically relevant quantum computer exists, so organizations
should deploy PQC or hybrid solutions for long-lived confidentiality as soon
as practical, and HNDL must be accounted for in migration timelines. Hybrid
(classical + PQC) deployments are endorsed during the transition. Status:
Initial Public Draft (November 12, 2024); as of mid-2026 the report is still
in draft, so the proposed dates are proposals, not final requirements.
