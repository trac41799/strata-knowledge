Detailed documented investigation (IEEE Computer, July 1993) of the Therac-25
computerized radiation therapy machine, which massively overdosed patients at
least six times between June 1985 and January 1987, causing severe injury or
death. Findings: the predecessor Therac-20 retained independent protective
circuits and mechanical interlocks, while the Therac-25 placed more safety
responsibility on software; overdoses occurred primarily because of software
errors combined with the manufacturer's failure to follow proper software
engineering practices (inadequate testing, failure to reproduce faults,
initial blame of hardware and operators). The canonical teaching case on
safety-critical software, professional responsibility, and organizational
factors in accidents.
