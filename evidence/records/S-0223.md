The paper that established formal software inspection: a structured,
meeting-based verification process with defined participant roles
(moderator, author, reader, recorder, inspectors), a fixed stage sequence
(planning, overview, preparation, inspection meeting, rework, follow-up),
and checklists/standards used to focus preparation. Industrial experience
report from IBM: substantial quality and productivity improvements,
including productivity gains of 20% or more, defect-cost escalation
(errors detected during system testing cost about 10x more to fix than
during construction, 10-25x more when detected post-release), and
learning effects (designers and programmers improve their own work
through participating in inspections). The historical root of all
subsequent review process models; still the reference point for formal
inspection. Web-verified via Wikipedia/IfSQ/IBM citations and the
journal record (accessed 2026-08-19).
