Final (July 2025) revision of the NIST authentication guideline. Memorized-secret
verifiers SHALL store secrets in a form resistant to offline attacks: salted and
iteratively hashed verification secrets, using a suitable one-way key derivation
function that takes password, salt, and cost factor as inputs (PBKDF2 and Balloon
are named examples; a memory-hard function SHOULD be used). Look-up secrets below
112 bits of entropy SHALL be salted and hashed with salts of at least 32 bits
stored alongside the hash. Authentication assurance levels: AAL2 requires
multi-factor authentication using two different authenticator types; AAL3 requires
a hardware-based authenticator plus an authenticator providing phishing
(verifier-impersonation) resistance. Reauthentication (Sec. 5.2): at AAL2 an
overall reauthentication timeout SHOULD be no more than 24 hours and the inactivity
timeout SHOULD be no more than 1 hour; at AAL3 the overall timeout SHALL be no more
than 12 hours and inactivity SHOULD be no more than 15 minutes; sessions are
terminated when the limits are reached, and AAL3 reauthentication equals initial
authentication. Rate-limiting of failed attempts is required (Sec. 3.2.2).
