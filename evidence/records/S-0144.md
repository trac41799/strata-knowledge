White paper presenting the five object-oriented design principles later known
as SOLID — Single Responsibility, Open-Closed, Liskov Substitution, Interface
Segregation, Dependency Inversion — as tools for managing dependencies and
stability in the face of change. Argues principles should govern when and how
patterns are applied, rather than patterns being used for their own sake.
The SOLID acronym was coined around 2004 by Michael Feathers from the initial
letters of the five principles. Web-verified: Wikipedia (SOLID) states the
principles come from the 2000 paper and that Feathers coined the acronym
around 2004; multiple secondary sources (Baeldung, IONOS, Spring Framework
Guru) confirm the attribution.
