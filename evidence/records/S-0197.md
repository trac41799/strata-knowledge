The standard survey of the B-tree family and why it became the de
facto file-organization standard for disk-based indexes: balanced
multiway trees that minimize page accesses (tree height grows
logarithmically in the number of keys for a fixed node capacity),
cheap splitting/merging under insert/delete, and the B+-tree variant
that separates keys from data, stores records only in leaves, and
links leaves in sequence for efficient range scans. Contrasts B, B*,
and B+ variants with their costs.
