Verified from publisher record and paper: introduces the support-vector
network (SVM) as a learning machine for two-group classification that
nonlinearly maps input vectors into a very high-dimensional feature space and
constructs a linear decision surface there; special properties of the surface
(separation margin and support vectors) are argued to ensure high
generalization. Presents the algorithm, VC-dimension-based generalization
bounds, and experiments (including digit recognition and text classification).
Canonical source for maximum-margin classifiers and the kernel trick as
established practice.
