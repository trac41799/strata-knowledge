Defines Turing machines ("a-machines") as a formal model of what a human
computer does when executing an algorithm, constructs a universal machine
that simulates any other machine, and proves by diagonalization that the
halting problem is undecidable. Applies this to show the Entscheidungsproblem
— deciding first-order validity — is unsolvable. Equivalence with
lambda-definability is established in Turing's 1937 companion paper.
