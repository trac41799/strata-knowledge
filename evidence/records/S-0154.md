Foundational paper proposing the model: a software architecture is a set of
architectural elements (processing, data, and connecting elements) that have a
particular form, explicated by a set of rationale. Form is defined by the
properties of the elements and the relationships among them — the constraints on
the elements. The rationale provides the underlying basis for the architecture by
capturing the motivation for the choice of architectural style, the choice of
elements, and the form — making design decisions and their justification
first-class parts of an architecture. Web-verified: ACM DOI 10.1145/141874.141884;
Fielding's dissertation (roy.gbiv.com, Chapter 1) summarizes the model as cited;
UCI ICS 221 lecture slides reproduce the elements/form/rationale decomposition.
