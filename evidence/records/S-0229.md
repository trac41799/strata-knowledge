Early empirical study of real programs (a set of FORTRAN programs at
Stanford, including compilers and language processors): Knuth
instrumented the programs and found execution time highly concentrated —
e.g., a 214-executable-statement program spent 70% of its runtime in a
single inner loop; a 245-statement program also spent ~70% of its time in
one loop; a 1300-statement program spent about half its time in one
loop. The canonical empirical basis for measurement-first performance
work: runtime concentrates in a small fraction of code, so optimization
must be guided by measurement (profiling), not intuition. The 1974
"Structured Programming with go to Statements" paper builds on this
line of argument when it cautions against premature optimization.
