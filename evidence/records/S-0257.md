Large-scale experiment — over half a million runs of the C4.5 and Naive-Bayes
algorithms over real-world datasets — comparing accuracy-estimation methods for
model selection. Findings verified from the paper text: the resubstitution
(training-set) estimate is optimistically biased; among the methods studied,
ten-fold stratified cross-validation was the best choice for model selection
even when more folds were computationally affordable; bootstrap variants trade
lower variance for bias. Canonical empirical basis for train/test/validation
split practice and for why model selection must be done on held-out (or
cross-validated) data rather than training error.
