The experience report that introduced the "technical debt" metaphor — the first
known reference to the term. Web-verified (full text at c2.com/doc/oopsla92.html,
dated March 26, 1992; history and attribution cross-checked via
techdebtcost.com/history and multiple secondary sources): "Shipping first time
code is like going into debt. A little debt speeds development so long as it is
paid back promptly with a rewrite. ... The danger occurs when the debt is not
repaid. Every minute spent on not-quite-right code counts as interest on that
debt. Entire engineering organizations can be brought to a stand-still under the
debt load of an unconsolidated implementation, object-oriented or otherwise."
Cunningham used the metaphor to explain to management why the team needed time
to consolidate before adding features. Note: the original source is a short
experience report (anecdote); the record is typed practitioner (level 7) per the
L2 ruling that the metaphor is 30 years canonical — widely adopted industry
practice — which justifies the T3 tagging of technical-debt claims in
`operations/software-maintenance`.
