The founding paper of zero-knowledge proofs. Introduces interactive proof
systems and the notion of knowledge complexity, and defines zero-knowledge as
protocols whose knowledge complexity is zero: the verifier's view can be
simulated without access to the witness, so the verifier learns nothing beyond
the truth of the statement. Establishes the completeness / soundness /
zero-knowledge property set and gives concrete ZK protocols for
number-theoretic languages (e.g., quadratic residuosity) -- the first
"prove knowledge of a secret without revealing it" constructions.
